the desk your ai works at, and how i keep it clear

wyatt horton · november 13, 2026

i run my business from my phone. my content pipeline is automated: ai agents edit my phone videos and post them on a schedule. all of it comes down to one idea i keep coming back to. an ai can only work with what is on its desk.

the desk

the nerdy name is a context window. think of it as a desk. everything you have said in the chat sits on it, and when the desk gets full the oldest pages slide off the edge.

so the ai isn't ignoring you. the desk is just full. that's why a really long chat gets sloppy, and why a fresh chat that starts with a short summary often gives a sharper answer.

the ai reads in small chunks of text called tokens, kind of like tiles in a word game. the desk only fits so many tiles. a short clear ask beats a long ramble because every extra tile takes room that something useful could have used.

what i put on the desk first

three things go on first every time:

  • my goal
  • who it's for
  • one example of what good looks like

that example matters most. showing an ai one finished piece of work teaches it more than a paragraph of me describing what i want.

the trend i keep noticing

i keep seeing more ai tools that can work on their own for a while, and more of them rely on other companies behind the scenes. an agent is just an ai that takes actions for you instead of only answering. the more it can do, the more it matters what it can reach.

so before i hand any helper a job, i write down what it can touch and what it can't. i also keep a simple list of every tool my ai uses and what kind of information goes into each one. if i wouldn't be okay with a stranger reading it, it doesn't go in.

decide once

here's the personal part. every small decision costs a little energy. what to eat, when to work out, when to check messages. so i turn repeating decisions into rules. a rule is just a decision you made once while you were thinking clearly.

it's the same reason i give my ai helpers written instructions instead of explaining things from scratch each day. when one of my agents makes a mistake, i don't just tell it off. i write a rule so it doesn't happen again. the desk stays clear and so does my head.

and when a task feels heavy, i shrink the first step until it's almost silly. open the page and type the title. put your shoes on. once you've started, you're just continuing.

try this week

  • open a new chat and paste a short summary of what you're working on, then ask your question again
  • pick one decision you make every morning and turn it into a rule
  • write down the smallest possible first step for the thing you've been avoiding

your experience plus ai beats either one alone. the ai brings the speed. you bring the goal, the example and the rules.
