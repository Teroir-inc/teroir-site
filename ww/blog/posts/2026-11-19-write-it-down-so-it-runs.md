my content pipeline is automated: ai agents edit my phone videos and post them on a schedule. an agent is just an assistant that takes a goal and does the steps in order. people ask how i set that up from my phone and the answer is boring. i wrote everything down.

## an ai only knows what it has read
when people say an ai was trained, they mean it read a gigantic library of text and learned the patterns in it. it doesn't look things up like a search engine. it's more like a friend who read every cookbook but has never cooked.

that means it knows nothing about you until you tell it. and when an assistant says it remembers you, it usually means a note got saved somewhere and pasted back at the start of each chat. the model itself starts every conversation blank, like a co-worker with amnesia who reads a sticky note before every meeting.

so the quality of the memory is the quality of the note. short, specific and kept up to date. i give each of my ai helpers its own sticky note, so the one that writes captions isn't carrying around notes about editing.

## the trend i keep noticing
i keep seeing more ai tools that can take a goal and work through several steps on their own, even on a phone. that's exciting, and it's also why the written part matters more, not less. a tool that can do the steps still needs to know what done looks like.

the rule i use is simple. it can do the steps, but anything that sends a message or spends money waits for my yes. the notes tell it how i like things done and my yes keeps me in charge.

here's the test i give any business, mine included. if you're out for a week, does the work keep going? it only does if the steps live somewhere other than your head.

## the personal part
consistency is a setup problem, not a motivation problem. once the editing and the posting were handled, the only decision left for me was pressing record. i film in the same kind of spot, i decide my first line before the day starts, and that's it.

the same idea works on the people side. i learned to say what i want in one sentence, and then i follow up the next morning with three lines: what we talked about, what happens next, and by when. i did that before i signed my first seven-figure term sheet, and it's the same move as the sticky note. you write it down so the good part doesn't fade.

## one thing to try this week
pick one job you do over and over. write a short note about how you like it done, with one example of a finished version you're proud of. paste that note at the top of your next chat with ai and see how much closer the first try lands.

then keep the note. add a line every time you fix something. that little file is the system, and it keeps working when you're out hiking, at the beach or just away from your desk.
