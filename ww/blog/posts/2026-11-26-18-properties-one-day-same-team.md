a team i built a system for took on 18 new properties in one day. same team. no new hires. i watched it happen.

## the day it happened

my property system started as just a private tool for a single co-living operator in houston and dfw. in june that team took on 18 new properties in a single day.

from start to finish the portfolio grew from 11 properties to 29, and from 83 rooms to 218. the team stayed the same.

so how does a small team take on that much at once?

## what the numbers show

here is what grew while the team stayed the same size:

- weekly chores went from 53 to 147
- messages to members went from about 60 a week to about 500 a week, sent automatically
- tickets from member texts, reviews and staff were tracked and handled in one place

none of that is flashy. it's the stuff that piles up fast when you grow. with it in hand, the team could say yes to 18 houses in a day instead of saying wait.

## here's my take

i think the small shop down the street has a better shot now than it used to. it can keep up with the big chain.

a big company has more people. but more people means more meetings, more handoffs and more ways for things to slip. one owner with a phone and good ai help can move fast.

i run my business from my phone. my content pipeline is automated: ai agents edit my phone videos and post them on a schedule. i'm one guy, and that still blows my mind.

the edge isn't the tool. it's you. you know your customers, your town, your weird little rules. ai doesn't know any of that until you tell it. your know-how plus ai beats either one alone.

## one thing to try this week

this is a slow week for a lot of people. that makes it a great week to build.

1. write down every task you do over and over. the emails, the follow ups, the reorders.
2. pick one. write out what a good version looks like, in your own voice.
3. hand that sample to an ai tool and ask it to do the next one the same way.
4. every time it gets something wrong, write the mistake down, plus the rule that stops it next time. keep that list in a file you can read.

that last step is the one i'd never skip. my content pipeline keeps a list like that, and each mistake only shows up once.

start with one task. once it's done right without you, pick the next one. that's how a small team gets ready to say yes when a big day shows up.
